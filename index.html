Where Australia's tourism money lands, and who depends on it

Australians and visitors spent about $211 billion on tourism in 2024-25. This page follows that money: where it goes, who relies on it, and how it changed after COVID.

By Liam Dixon · FIT3179 Data Visualisation 2, Monash University · October 2026

1. The big picture 2. Where the money goes 3. Per resident 4. Over time About this page

1 · The big picture

Three states take four of every five dollars

Australians and visitors spent $211 billion on tourism in 2024-25.

NSW, Victoria and Queensland took $166 billion, or 79%.

Tasmania, the NT and the ACT together took just 5.6%.

Spend fell 36% in two years, from $148 billion to $94 billion.

By 2024-25 it was 43% above 2018-19. Some of that is higher prices, as the figures are not adjusted for inflation.

Overseas visitors are now 20% of spend, down from 25% in 2018-19.

The recovery came from Australians. Day-trip spend nearly doubled, from $23 billion to $44 billion.

Overseas spend matters most in NSW (24%) and Victoria (22%), least in Tasmania (14%).

TakeawayAustralia's tourism money is concentrated in three states, and the recovery was led by Australians.

2 · Where the money goes

Spend is concentrated, but so are people

Ten of the 76 regions take 68% of all spend.

The 40 smallest regions take just 9%.

Sydney and Melbourne take 33% between them, about $70 billion.

No other region reaches $19 billion.

They also hold 36% of Australia's people.

So their spend is not unusual. Per head, about $7,000, they sit below the national $7,650.

Perth takes 70% of Western Australia's tourist spend.

Brisbane takes only 38% of Queensland's. The Gold Coast adds $10 billion, and 11 more regions share the rest.

TakeawayMoney piles up in the big cities, but most big-city regions get less per resident.

3 · Per resident

Small places depend on visitors the most

Per resident, the picture flips. The 23 regions with under 50,000 people average $12,900 a head.

The 9 regions with over 500,000 people average $7,300.

Lasseter, around Uluru, takes $181,050 per resident: $423 million spread over 2,337 people.

Tasmania's East Coast ($36,820), the Whitsundays ($30,954) and Kangaroo Island ($28,014) follow.

The Gold Coast ($14,939) is the only big region in the top ten, at double Brisbane's $6,720.

Smaller regions tend to take more per resident. Eight of the nine regions over 500,000 people are under $10,000 a head.

The exception is the Gold Coast.

TakeawayPer resident, small remote regions depend on visitors the most. Uluru is the extreme.

4 · Over time

The recovery came from Australians, not overseas visitors

Every state earns more than in 2018-19. South Australia grew 55%, Queensland and the NT 51%.

The ACT grew least, at 28%.

Overseas visitors are the missing piece. Their spend fell 97% in 2020-21 and is only 14% above 2018-19.

NSW ranks first for overseas spend every year.

Queensland (+5%), Tasmania (+6%) and the NT (−6%) have barely recovered. SA (+43%) and WA (+34%) are well ahead.

In 2020-21 Queensland passed Victoria, whose total spend fell 52%.

In 2020-21 the big cities fell furthest: Melbourne to 27, Sydney to 35.

Holiday and regional areas held up better. North Coast NSW stayed at 88 and Central NSW at 87.

By 2023-24 all 20 regions were above their 2018-19 level.

This heatmap uses the 2023-24 data release only. Output is not adjusted for inflation.

TakeawayEvery state is ahead of 2018-19, but overseas visitors have not fully returned.

So what

Concentrated, uneven, and still shifting

Tourism money lands in a few big places: Sydney and Melbourne take a third. Per resident it flips, and small regions like Lasseter, the East Coast and the Whitsundays rely on visitors far more. The recovery since COVID came from Australians, so places that lean on overseas visitors still have ground to make up.

Data sources

  • Tourism Research Australia (TRA), State and regional tourism satellite accounts 2024-25: regional spend, and state series from 2018-19 to 2024-25.
  • TRA, Regional Tourism Satellite Account 2023-24: regional output (GVA), 2016-17 to 2023-24.
  • Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS): regional population estimates and tourism region boundaries.

Things to know

  • The 2023-24 and 2024-25 releases use different region lists. I do not join them into one series. Region-level time charts use 2023-24 data only.
  • The maps use 2021 tourism region boundaries. A few regions may not match the newest boundaries exactly.
  • Money figures are in current dollars, not adjusted for inflation.
  • "Per resident" means tourist spend divided by resident population. Small regions can look extreme because the divisor is small.
